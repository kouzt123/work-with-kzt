# Influencer Marketing

## Open-Core Scope

This module fully covers the analytical layer: creator vetting, shortlist review, brief quality control, script diagnosis, and campaign-fit assessment.

It does not require private creator databases, employer-specific workflows, or platform-internal data. Use public information, creator-provided information, and tools available in the host environment. If an important data point is unavailable, say so and state what it would change.

## What This Module Does

Choose creators as both media channels and creative partners. A strong creator is not simply the largest account: their audience, current distribution, content modes, commercial economics, and ability to integrate the product all have to work together.

Start with the verdict: recommend, reject, or investigate further. Then explain the evidence and the execution implications.

## Vet Creators on Objective and Subjective Evidence

### 1. Read the relationships between the numbers

Review follower count, views, and engagement, but do not stop at the absolute values. Look at their relationship to each other.

- A large follower base with consistently weak views can indicate accumulated historical influence but weaker current heat. It is not an automatic rejection, but the expected reach, price, and role in the campaign must reflect it.
- Assess commercial price primarily through expected CPM based on actual or expected views, not follower count alone.
- Use platform-native audience data where available and third-party estimates as supporting evidence, not as unquestioned truth.

The point of measurement is to understand what the numbers mean. Tools and plugins can calculate ratios; judgment is needed to decide whether a creator's current distribution and price are commercially sensible.

### 2. Understand the audience beyond the dashboard

Audience data matters, but experienced category judgment adds information that a dashboard often misses. A gaming creator's game choices, for example, reveal likely audience interests and expectations. Platform algorithms also create predictable audience patterns by repeatedly distributing certain content types to certain viewers.

Use both:

- Available audience demographics and platform insights.
- The creator's recurring content, community, and the likely audience associated with each content type.
- Community interviews, peer feedback on competitor collaborations, and performance from relevant paid placements as secondary evidence.

### 3. Judge the product and creative fit

Ask two subjective but material questions:

1. Does this creator's tone and public identity fit the product?
2. Can their content style integrate the product naturally?

A creator can be popular and highly capable while still being a poor commercial choice if the sponsored content will feel forced. Do not choose based on polish, fame, or a surface-level demographic match alone.

## Set the Right Brand-Creator Boundary

The brand owns product truth: how the product works, which claims are accurate, what the campaign needs to achieve, and the non-negotiable product experience.

The creator should usually own the creative expression. If the point of the collaboration is to benefit from their relationship with their audience, give them meaningful room to decide how the content feels and how the idea is expressed.

Use a demo or inspiration piece to show a credible product-content combination. It should prevent the work from drifting away from the product, not force the creator to reproduce the brand's exact execution.

There is one important distinction:

- **Audience-led collaboration:** Trust the creator's knowledge of what their audience will accept; protect product accuracy while leaving room for their creative judgment.
- **Talent-as-production collaboration:** If the creator is primarily hired for their appearance or on-camera execution rather than their audience influence, the brand can reasonably control more of the content.

Use a good brief template so routine information is not omitted. A template clarifies responsibilities and boundaries; it does not eliminate the need for judgment about the specific creator and product combination.

## Diagnose Proposals and Scripts

Evaluate a creator proposal or script on two dimensions:

### 1. Is the creative native to this creator?

Look for whether the concept belongs on the creator's channel and reflects how they actually make content.

- Question a visibly copied idea or a format that would feel alien to the creator's audience. It is unlikely to perform just because it worked elsewhere, and may introduce originality or rights concerns.
- Some creators operate more than one content mode. A creator may publish cosplay and non-cosplay work, for example, with different audiences and performance patterns. Treat these as different distribution choices, then discuss which mode matches the campaign audience.

### 2. Does the product integrate naturally and accurately?

The proposal should show the full intended video journey, including how the product enters the story and what the audience is meant to understand or do. It does not need to be perfect on the first pass.

If the creator can clearly explain the sequence and the concept is not fundamentally off-track, work with it. The brand cannot know every creative convention in every vertical; leave room to test, learn, and refine with the partner.

## Decision Guide

| Signal | What it means | Appropriate response |
| --- | --- | --- |
| High followers but weak current views | Historical reach may not equal present distribution. | Reassess expected views, CPM, and campaign role before proceeding. |
| Strong metrics but forced product fit | The paid content may not feel credible to the audience. | Do not select on numbers alone; look for a more natural fit or different content mode. |
| A creator has several distinct content styles | Each style can reach different audiences and produce different results. | Choose the style deliberately; do not assume the account is one homogeneous audience. |
| An original, clear concept with imperfect execution detail | The partner understands the assignment and may be workable. | Give focused feedback and allow iteration. |
| An entertaining concept that misstates or obscures the product | Entertainment is not solving the commercial job. | Redesign the product integration before approval. |

## Output Format

Use a concise creator memo:

1. **Verdict** - Recommend, reject, or investigate further.
2. **Media quality** - Follower/view/engagement relationships, expected views, and CPM logic.
3. **Audience fit** - Available data, content-derived audience judgment, and relevant algorithm expectations.
4. **Product and creative fit** - Why the integration should feel natural, or why it may fail.
5. **Collaboration model** - What the brand controls, what the creator owns, and the demo or inspiration needed.
6. **Proposal or script review** - Native creative, product integration, questions, and revision direction.
7. **Risks and missing evidence** - Unverified assumptions, availability, rights, pricing, or measurement gaps.

## Questions To Ask When Material Information Is Missing

- What is the campaign goal: awareness, product education, conversion, content production, or something else?
- What does the product require the creator to show or explain accurately?
- Is the collaboration audience-led or mainly using the creator as production talent?
- What are the creator's recent views, expected views, fee, and planned deliverables?
- Which content modes exist on the account, and which audience does each one reach?
- What audience data, past branded examples, and partner-availability signals are available?

## Guardrails

- Avoid ad-speak and fake enthusiasm.
- Do not mistake follower count for current reach or commercial value.
- Do not assume a polished creator is a natural product fit.
- Do not give the creator responsibility for discovering how a complex product works.
- Do not over-control creative expression when the collaboration depends on the creator's audience relationship.
- Do not approve a copied or non-native idea merely because it looks familiar.
- Do not invent private benchmarks, platform-internal signals, creator availability, or projected results.
- Keep all examples generic and public-safe. Do not include employer-internal frameworks, data, or non-public information.
