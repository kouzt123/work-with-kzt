# Creative Production

## What This Module Does

Turn an approved marketing strategy into accurate, scalable creative output. Use it to prepare production packages, brief creators and vendors, review first drafts, and build a production system that gets faster without losing the product truth.

The strategy owner is responsible for more than an idea. They need to know the product well enough to answer the production team's real questions, much as a product manager needs to make a product spec usable for an engineering team.

## Core Principle: Product Truth Cannot Be Fully Outsourced

The people closest to the product must collect and define the source material before external production begins. Do not expect an external studio, designer, or creator to discover the product's real value alone.

Prepare a product source pack that can include:

- Screen recordings of key flows.
- Documented use cases and outcomes.
- Clear instructions for how the product works.
- Approved claims, limitations, and non-negotiable product details.
- Product assets appropriate to the production format.

The pack changes by need:

- **Static design:** Relevant product UI and visual-design assets.
- **Video:** Product models, 3D assets, screen recordings, and the required product moments.
- **Creator collaborations:** A practical demo guide and a defined path from setup to use to outcome.

Do not hand a complex product to a creator and expect deep discovery. A top creator's team may only have an hour or two to understand it before moving on. Give them the essential product experience in a form they can use immediately.

## Translate Strategy Into the Production Language

Strategy communication is the work of translating a marketing decision into instructions that each production partner can act on.

### Creator work

- Explain the product truth, audience, intended selling point, and non-negotiables.
- Make room for the creator's own style; ask for a proposal that shows how the product will fit naturally into it.
- Give a concise demo path rather than a generic product document.

### Static design

- Specify the layout intent, the selling-point integration, and the visual information the image must convey.
- Provide the actual UI or source assets needed to make the claim believable.

### Video production

- Specify the script, hook, selling point, and call to action for that particular video.
- For brand-led work, define the intended message at a frame-by-frame level when that precision matters.
- Do not reuse a generic requirement sheet across every video; the intended job of each video can differ.

## Review First Drafts by Diagnosing the Failure

Initial drafts often need work. Separate the problem before giving feedback:

| What happened | Decision |
| --- | --- |
| The output does not reflect the brief or product at all. | Send it back for a fundamental rework. |
| The brief and product are understood, but the creative execution is weak or unnatural. | Preserve the correct parts and focus the revision on the weak ones. |
| The work is entertaining, but the product is misunderstood or poorly integrated. | Redesign the product expression before relying on the entertainment value. |

Do not treat all poor drafts as the same failure. The right revision depends on whether the partner missed the requirement, lacks a creative solution, or needs help integrating the product.

## Build Partners, Not Just Deliverables

Communication and attitude affect whether a partner becomes a scalable production resource. Identify each collaborator's strengths and weaknesses, then give feedback that helps them improve.

Where a partner misses a quality floor, make the floor concrete. For example, provide a short workflow or tutorial for a suitable audio or voice-production tool instead of repeatedly saying that the sound is not good enough. The goal is not an endless approval loop; it is a partner who can meet the standard independently after a few rounds.

Speed is a production advantage. When partners understand the product, quality bar, and feedback pattern, they need fewer client approvals and can reliably produce volume. That creates more opportunities to learn and scale.

## Build a Creative Matrix for Scale

Plan asset variation on two axes:

1. **Selling point:** The product benefit, use case, or reason to choose.
2. **Creative packaging:** The format or expression used to make the idea interesting and understandable.

Most production should vary both axes. Do not put the entire plan behind one creative unless there is a special reason.

- **Creative packaging** should be explored for every product. The same message can work or fail depending on how it is expressed.
- **Selling points** should be explored when the product supports several credible jobs or benefits. This matters especially for social and AI products. A simple discount-led offer may have a more stable selling point, while a broader product can justify several benefit tests.

The exception is a high-cost, high-stakes creator collaboration. When a direction has already been confirmed and the partner is expensive, it can be appropriate to scale the proven selling point and packaging rather than introduce unnecessary variation.

Before launch, translate the media plan into production capacity: the required volume of assets, creators, concepts, and content needed to support the intended budget. Do not use a generic content-count formula; the necessary volume depends on the product, channel, budget, creator model, and approval speed.

## Output Format

Use a production-ready brief:

1. **Objective** - The audience, market, channel, and business job for the asset.
2. **Product source pack** - Required recordings, UI, demos, claims, and product guardrails.
3. **Creative direction** - Selling point, packaging, hook, product integration, and call to action.
4. **Format-specific instructions** - Creator, static, or video requirements.
5. **Production matrix** - What varies across the batch and what remains fixed.
6. **Owners and dependencies** - Who supplies assets, produces, reviews, and approves; what must be validated locally.
7. **Review criteria** - Brief alignment, product comprehension, creative quality, and quality floor.
8. **Capacity plan** - Needed content, creator, and concept volume, with stated assumptions.

## Questions To Ask When Material Information Is Missing

- What product flows, use cases, and assets can be shown publicly and accurately?
- Which parts of the product must appear in the asset for the claim to be credible?
- Who is producing each format, and how much product context will they realistically have?
- What selling points and packaging formats should be tested or held fixed?
- Which partner abilities need enablement before scale?
- What content volume is required for the budget and channel plan?

## Guardrails

- Do not delegate product understanding entirely to external producers.
- Do not use the same brief format for creators, static designers, and video producers.
- Do not approve entertaining content that leaves the product incoherent.
- Do not mistake repeated review comments for a scalable quality system; give partners a usable standard and enablement.
- Do not assume one successful creative should receive all of the budget unless the context justifies it.
- Do not invent product claims, asset-volume targets, or production constraints.
- Keep examples generic and public-safe. Do not include employer-internal frameworks, data, or non-public information.
